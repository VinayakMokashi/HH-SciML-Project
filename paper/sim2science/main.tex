\begin{abstract}
A conductance-based neuron model is written one current at a time, and at least
one of those currents is usually unknown. The standard remedy has two stages: a
universal differential equation keeps the trusted kinetics and puts a neural
network where the unknown current belongs, and a symbolic step then reads a
conductance off the learned term. The first stage is tested constantly; the
second is usually taken on trust. We ran both on a six-state Hodgkin--Huxley
neuron with a hidden calcium current, from one short noisy trajectory, against
ground truth we controlled. Stage one earns its reputation: the closure tracks
held-out voltage across the whole remainder of the trace and reproduces the
hidden current's shape. Stage two does not. The conductance read off that closure
is only order-correct and moves by more than a factor of two across seeds, while
forecast error, $R^2$ and training loss stay good and give no warning. The usual
explanation is that one short trajectory does not carry enough information to pin
a hidden current's conductance. It does: a direct two-parameter fit to the same
trajectory, with no network anywhere, returns the conductance to about six per
cent. What fails is the practical recovery of the physical coefficient from
this flexible closure, under the tested objectives and distillation procedures:
parametric identifiability holds, and the functional identifiability of this
closure, under both the distillations we ran, does not.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Conductance-based models describe a neuron as a sum of ionic currents with their
own gating kinetics \citep{hodgkin1952}. The well-characterised currents are
known; the slower modulatory ones that shape bursting and adaptation are usually
not. These models are also degenerate: very different conductance sets produce
indistinguishable activity \citep{prinz2004, marder2011}, averaging across cells
can yield a model matching none of them \citep{golowasch2002}, and wide parameter
variation leaves function intact \citep{ori2018}. A good fit is therefore weak
evidence about the physics.

Universal differential equations offer a way through
\citep{rackauckas2020universal}: keep the trusted terms, replace the unknown one
with a neural network, fit through the solver. They are being adopted across
systems biology \citep{philipps2025ude} and proposed as a unifying modelling
language for neuroscience \citep{elgazzar2025ude}; the black-box limit is the
neural ODE \citep{chen2018neural}, the physics-constrained limit a
physics-informed network \citep{raissi2019pinn}. Their appeal beyond prediction
is that the unknown term is isolated as one learned function, so a second step
can distil it into closed form by sparse regression
\citep{brunton2016sindy, rudy2017pdefind} or symbolic search
\citep{udrescu2020aifeynman, cranmer2023pysr} and the coefficients read as
biophysics -- here, a conductance and a reversal potential.
\citet{beck2026hybrid} learn unknown currents inside such models, and a
domain-specific recurrent network recovers hidden ionic
states \citep{iravanian2020}.

That last step carries the scientific weight and is checked least often. It
assumes the data determine the coefficient, and identifiability analysis says
that needs testing: gating variables and conductances of a generalised
Hodgkin--Huxley model are not separately identifiable from voltage-clamp
recordings \citep{walch2016}, the classical model needs reparametrising before it
can be fitted at all \citep{daly2015}, and structural and practical
identifiability are routinely distinguished \citep{raue2009, wieland2021}. Recent
work draws the distinction that matters here, between an identifiable
\emph{parameter} and an identifiable unknown \emph{function} standing in for the
same physics \citep{loman2026structural, loman2025ude, norden2025structural}.
Discovery practice has not caught up, and \citet{silvestri2023analysis} find UDE
recovery fragile even where it is possible.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\textbf{We audit the two-stage predict-then-interpret recipe against ground truth
we control, and the stage normally taken on trust is the one that fails.} Stage
one holds; stage two does not, and not for the reason usually given: a direct fit
to the same trajectory pins the conductance to about six per cent while the
closure's estimate scatters roughly six times wider, and a matched-objective
control charges the larger share of that gap to the loss function.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\paragraph{Data generation.}
The generator is a Hodgkin--Huxley membrane equation \citep{hodgkin1952} with
sodium, potassium and leak currents, a persistent sodium current
\citep{magistretti1999, golomb1997}, and a high-threshold calcium current
$I_{Ca}=g_{Ca}s^2(V-E_{Ca})$ driven by one activation gate $s$
\citep{pospischil2008, reuveni1993}. Five gates ($m$, $h$, $n$, $p$, $s$) with
the voltage give six states, integrated over $[0,\Tfull]$\,ms from rest at
$V_0=\Vrest$\,mV \citep{rackauckas2017diffeq}. The headline $g_{Ca}=\gcaMain$ is
five times the physiological \gcaPhys, which is derived rather than tuned by
rescaling a published bursting pyramidal cell's calcium density to this model's
squid-axon sodium conductance; App.~\ref{app:eqns} gives that arithmetic and
lists every model constant with its provenance.

\paragraph{Preprocessing.}
The trajectory is sampled at \Ndata{} uniform points and every channel corrupted
with Ornstein--Uhlenbeck noise at \NoisePct\,\% of that channel's own standard
deviation. Training uses the $\Ntrain$ samples with $t\le\Ttrain$\,ms; every
forecast number is the tail of the same integration, scored against the
\emph{clean} trajectory. We run $\valUdeForecastVRmseN$ seeds, each redrawing
noise and initialisation jointly.

\paragraph{Neural ODE baseline.}
The black-box baseline replaces the whole vector field with a network
($\ArchNode$), trained \AdamNode{} Adam iterations plus a \BfgsNode-iteration
BFGS polish. As originally configured it read raw time as an input alongside the
six states.

\paragraph{Universal differential equation.}
The UDE keeps every known term and the exact gate kinetics, replacing only the
calcium current with a multilayer perceptron $U(\NNinputs)$ of $\NparamUde$
parameters \citep{pal2023lux}. Training is Adam for \AdamUde{} iterations then a
BFGS polish of \BfgsUde{} \citep{dixit2023optimization} under a continuous
adjoint \citep{ma2021comparison}. The loss is a sum of squares over the sampled
states in native units,
${\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{full}} = \sum_{i=1}^{\Ntrain}\sum_{c=1}^{6}\bigl(x_c(t_i)-\hat{x}_c(t_i)\bigr)^2}$,
with no normalisation and no channel weights. Voltage's standard deviation
exceeds the mean gate standard deviation by more than $\GateSdRatio\times$, so at
the noise floor voltage carries \LossShareV\,\% of the expected value of this
unweighted objective: it is already almost a voltage-only objective.

\paragraph{Symbolic distillation.}
The trained closure is distilled by sparse regression \emph{constrained to} the
conductance form $s^2(aV+b)$ on the supervised trajectory. The form is
\emph{supplied} to the estimator, not found by it, so what follows is agreement
with an assumed form and not discovery of one; $a$ is the conductance and $-b/a$
the reversal potential.
% claim: sindy-does-not-sparsify
A second, unconstrained sparse regression over a six-term library on the same
closure never sparsifies and still scores
$R^2=\valSymbolicRTwoSindyTrueMeanStd$ against the true current
(App.~\ref{app:hyper}).

\subsection{Stage two: the conductance does not come back}
\label{sec:parametric}

% claim: symbolic-form-recovered
% claim: symbolic-coefficients-not-identifiable
Given the conductance form, the distilled closure reproduces the true calcium
current with $R^2=\valSymbolicRTwoTrajTrainMeanStd$ on the supervised trajectory
and describes the network's own output about as well
($R^2=\valSymbolicRTwoNnTrajTrainMeanStd$). The shape is right; the scale is not.
The conductance that same fit implies is
$\hat{a}=\valSymbolicAHatTrajTrainMeanStd$ against a true \gcaMain, spanning
\valSymbolicAHatRangeTrajTrain{} across seeds, wide enough that a single run
would licence almost any conclusion.

% claim: parametric-fit-identifies-conductance
The natural reading is that one short noisy trajectory does not carry enough
information about a hidden current to pin its conductance. That is testable, and
it does not survive. We fit $(g_{Ca},E_{Ca})$ to the \emph{same} noisy
trajectory, true functional form known and no network in the loop, minimising a
noise-weighted $\chi^2$ by multi-start Nelder--Mead. It returns
$g_{Ca}=\valParamFitGcaHatTwoMeanStd$ against a true \gcaMain, a relative spread
of about six per cent, at $\chi^2$ per point of $\valParamFitChiSqPerPointTwo$,
near unity and so at the known noise floor. The pre-registered baseline seed's
estimate is the lowest of the five, and the 95\% profile interval centred on % numok: 95% confidence level, a convention
\emph{that} estimate is $[1.6065, 2.1477]$; each realisation owns its own. % numok: Delta-chi2 = 3.8415 crossings, linearly interpolated between the bracketing grid points of results/identifiability/profile_gca_gca2.0.csv
Four of the five conductances distilled from the closure fall clearly outside
that interval and the fifth sits on its lower edge (Fig.~\ref{fig:ident}).

% claim: closure-induced-nonidentifiability
So the trajectory determines the conductance and the closure is what loses it. A
two-parameter model handed the true form \emph{should} beat a
$\NparamUde$-parameter closure handed none, and it forecasts about twice as well
(Table~\ref{tab:main}), so the claim is not that two equally good fits disagree.
Nor are the two spreads the same kind of quantity: $\hat g_{Ca}$ is a parameter
the likelihood estimates directly, while $\hat a$ is a least-squares projection
of an already-learned function, so its scatter compounds what the closure learned
with what that projection discards. We read the gap as a contrast in kind, not
one error bar against another. It runs one way, and that is enough: because some
estimator recovers the conductance to six per cent from this trajectory, the
closure's scatter cannot be charged to the data.

\subsection{What owns the gap, and what does not close it}
\label{sec:decomp}

% claim: closure-induced-nonidentifiability
The data were held fixed across that comparison, but the representation was not
the only thing that changed: the parametric arm minimises a noise-weighted
$\chi^2$ by multi-start Nelder--Mead, while the closure descends the unweighted
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{full}}$, which is \LossShareV\,\% voltage at the noise floor,
by Adam and BFGS. So we repeat the parametric fit under
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{full}}$ -- same data, same
true form, same two free parameters, same starting points, changing nothing but
which residual is minimised. It returns $\valParamFitGcaHatTwoSseFullMeanStd$, a
relative spread of roughly twenty per cent against six under the likelihood. The
objective therefore accounts for about a threefold inflation on its own, and the
closure's roughly forty per cent sits a further twofold above \emph{that}. That
control swaps the objective while keeping multi-start Nelder--Mead, so it cannot
separate the representation from the optimiser: the residual twofold bounds the
representation's own contribution rather than measuring it, and it is the figure
to quote, never the sixfold one, which is the two effects compounded.

% claim: retrain-improves-loss-worsens-recovery
Nor had the closure simply failed to converge. Quadrupling the budget to
\AdamRe/\BfgsRe{} lowers the training objective from $\valTrainLossBefore$ to
$\valTrainLossAfter$, about \LossDropPct\,per cent, and makes every recovery
quantity worse. Both symbolic quantities are quoted on the \emph{convex-hull}
domain, where the retrained closures were distilled, so that pair is like-for-like
and its before-values differ from the supervised-trajectory figures above. On the
hull the conductance moves further from the truth and its spread inflates
($\hat{a}$: $\valSymbolicAHatMeanStd \to \valRetrainAHatMeanStd$) and agreement
with the true current falls
($R^2$: $\valSymbolicRTwoCondTrueMeanStd \to \valRetrainRTwoCondTrueMeanStd$);
even the trajectory forecast, which has no fitting domain, degrades
($\valUdeForecastVRmseMeanStd \to \valRetrainForecastVRmseMeanStd$\,mV). At the
true structure's maximum-likelihood $(g_{Ca},E_{Ca})$ the identical objective
scores $\valParamFitSseFullTwo$, so both training losses sit \emph{below} the
correct physics on the data the physics generated.

% claim: gca-sweep-shows-nonidentifiability
Nor is $\gcaMain$ chosen to flatter the method: sweeping $g_{Ca}$ over
$\{\gcaPhys,\gcaMid,\gcaMain,\gcaHi\}$ at $\valUdeForecastVRmseN$ seeds per point
leaves forecast voltage error and raw calcium error flat while the
\emph{normalised} error falls from $\valGcaSweepICaNormPointFourMeanStd$ to
$\valGcaSweepICaNormFourMeanStd$. Raising the conductance does not improve the
closure, it enlarges the target. Only the $\gcaMid\!\to\!\gcaMain$ step clears
two pooled standard deviations, so we claim the trend and not the steps.

\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

% claim: predictive-accuracy-is-not-identifiability
The recipe's first stage does what it promises: extrapolation stays accurate on
every seed across the remainder of the trajectory, and it yields a closure
agreeing closely with the true calcium current's shape. The second stage does not
return that current's conductance, and not because the trajectory lacks the
information: a direct parametric fit to the same trajectory recovers the
conductance to about six per cent, so the pipeline loses what the data carry.
No diagnostic a practitioner would normally trust gives any warning. Forecast
error is sub-millivolt, $R^2$ against the true current is
$\valSymbolicRTwoTrajTrainMeanStd$, and the training loss is not merely low but
lower than the correctly fitted true structure's own loss.

% claim: identifiability-is-the-general-lesson
We state the general form of that as a recommendation, not a result, because our
evidence is one system. Where a UDE's learned term is to be interpreted rather
than simulated, its identifiability should be established before its coefficients
are read as physical quantities, and the question is not only whether the data
determine the parameter but whether they still determine it once a flexible
closure carries it. Two cheap diagnostics follow: a seed-spread check on the
recovered coefficients, which would have caught this failure immediately, and,
where the functional form is approximately known, a parametric fit to compare
against, which turns a bare spread into an attribution.

% =========================================================================
\section{Limitations and future work}
\label{sec:limits}

Running the conductance at \gcaMain{} makes the hidden current easier to see than
in a real cell; at \gcaPhys{} the closure learns essentially nothing, on the one
seed distilled there, while the direct fit recovers it across all five.
Everything here is one model, one trajectory family and one closure architecture,
so ``flexibility costs identifiability'' is demonstrated rather than established
in general. No statistical test is run anywhere and $\valUdeForecastVRmseN$ seeds
would support almost none, so every comparison here is an equivalence-within-noise
statement and not an ordering.

% claim: closure-induced-nonidentifiability
We also did not vary the distillation. Two methods were run on the same trained
closure and both fail, differently: the constrained fit returns a scattered
conductance, the unconstrained six-term regression never sparsifies. Given the
closure's output, the constrained fit's two coefficients are the least-squares
optimum in a basis well conditioned on the supervised trajectory (condition
number $\valSymbolicCondTrajTrain$), so that arm has no room left to be at fault
and the scatter originates in the closure. A different inductive bias could still
change the answer, by searching a different form or constraining $E_{Ca}$, and we
have not tried one. We therefore claim the failure for this closure under these
distillations, not for closure-based recovery in general. Locating where
identifiability is lost needs a ladder of intermediate closures between two
parameters and the full network: the obvious next experiment.

\section{Model constants, kinetics and the physiological reference}
\label{app:eqns}

The classical gating kinetics are taken verbatim from \citet{hodgkin1952} in the
resting-potential-referenced convention:
\begin{align*}
  \alpha_m(V) &= \frac{0.1\,(V+40)}{1-e^{-(V+40)/10}}, &  % numok: HH kinetics, src/hh_core.jl:50-63
  \beta_m(V)  &= 4\,e^{-(V+65)/18}, \\                    % numok: HH kinetics, src/hh_core.jl:50-63
  \alpha_h(V) &= 0.07\,e^{-(V+65)/20}, &                  % numok: HH kinetics, src/hh_core.jl:50-63
  \beta_h(V)  &= \frac{1}{1+e^{-(V+35)/10}}, \\           % numok: HH kinetics, src/hh_core.jl:50-63
  \alpha_n(V) &= \frac{0.01\,(V+55)}{1-e^{-(V+55)/10}}, & % numok: HH kinetics, src/hh_core.jl:50-63
  \beta_n(V)  &= 0.125\,e^{-(V+65)/80},                   % numok: HH kinetics, src/hh_core.jl:50-63
\end{align*}
with the removable singularities of $\alpha_m,\alpha_n$ evaluated at their
analytic limits. The two added gates use steady-state activations
$p_\infty(V) = [1+e^{-(V+50)/6}]^{-1}$ and $s_\infty(V) = [1+e^{-(V+25)/9}]^{-1}$ % numok: p_inf/s_inf, src/hh_core.jl:65,70
with first-order relaxation at fixed time constants $\tau_p,\tau_s$. Both are
logistic approximations, not kinetics transcribed from a source: the
high-threshold calcium activation of \citet{reuveni1993} is specified by
voltage-dependent rate functions whose steady state sits lower and rises more
steeply than the single logistic used here. Nothing reported in this paper
depends on the shape of $s_\infty$: the gate kinetics are known physics,
supplied identically to the data generator, to the closure and to the parametric
fit, so they cancel from every comparison we draw, and the recovery target is the
pair $(g_{Ca},E_{Ca})$, not the activation curve. Of the four added-gate
activation constants only the persistent-sodium half-point is
literature-anchored, and Table~\ref{tab:params} labels the other three as
choices. The persistent-sodium \emph{conductance} $g_{NaP}=\mgNaP$ is likewise a
declared choice: it is of the same order as the persistent-sodium conductance of
\citet{golomb1997} once rescaled to our sodium conductance, but we did not verify
a printed value against the original and prefer to mark it as ours than to
attribute a number we have not checked. It is a constant of the ground-truth
generator, supplied identically to the data, the closure and the parametric fit,
so it too cancels from every comparison.

\paragraph{The physiological reference value.}
\citet{pospischil2008} model the same \citet{reuveni1993} high-threshold calcium
current in an intrinsically-bursting cortical pyramidal cell at a density of
\gcaLit\,\nicefrac{mS}{cm$^2$} alongside a sodium density of
\gNaLit\,\nicefrac{mS}{cm$^2$} (their published implementation, ModelDB accession
123623). % numok: literature densities, demo_PY_IB.hoc:133,137
Our model inherits the classical squid-axon $g_{Na}=\mgNa$\,\nicefrac{mS}{cm$^2$}
rather than their cortical value, so the quantity to carry across is the
\emph{ratio} of calcium to sodium conductance and not the absolute density.
Holding that ratio fixed gives $(\gcaLit/\gNaLit)\times\mgNa =
\gcaRescaled$\,\nicefrac{mS}{cm$^2$}, which we round to \gcaPhys{} and call
physiological throughout. The headline \gcaMain{} is five times that.

\section{Hyperparameters, training and metric definitions}
\label{app:hyper}

\paragraph{Architectures and optimisation.}
The UDE calcium closure is a multilayer perceptron $\ArchUde$ with $\tanh$
activations and inputs $\NNinputs$ ($\NparamUde$ parameters); the black-box
neural ODE is $\ArchNode$ with layer normalisation and inputs of the six
normalised states and raw time ($\approx\NparamNode$ parameters). Both are
integrated with an explicit fifth-order Runge--Kutta method at absolute and
relative tolerances of $\Tol$, under an interpolating continuous adjoint with
reverse-mode vector--Jacobian products. Training is two-stage: Adam at learning
rate $\Lr$ followed by a BFGS polish (initial step-norm $\Lr$), wrapped so that a
failed polish degrades to the Adam result. Budgets are Adam \AdamUde/BFGS
\BfgsUde{} for the UDE and Adam \AdamNode/BFGS \BfgsNode{} for the neural ODE as
originally configured; the two repaired neural-ODE rows of Table~\ref{tab:main}
use the UDE's budget instead, and the autonomous one drops the time input so its
first layer is $\ArchNodeAuto$. The aggressive-retrain control of
Sec.~\ref{sec:decomp} uses Adam \AdamRe/BFGS \BfgsRe. The seeds are
$\{1111,2222,3333,4444,5555\}$; % numok: seed set, experiments_runner.jl:57
single-run panels use the first of them, fixed before the spread was known and
checked afterwards to lie inside the five-seed inter-quartile range.

\paragraph{The two distillations.}
The constrained fit is a sparse regression restricted to the supplied
conductance form $s^2(aV+b)$, evaluated on the supervised trajectory; its design
matrix has condition number $\valSymbolicCondTrajTrain$ there, so its two
coefficients are the least-squares optimum in a well-conditioned basis. The
unconstrained arm is a six-term polynomial sparse regression over the same
trained closure, fitted on a hull-masked grid spanning the training trajectory.
It retains every thresholded term at every seed and fits the physical $Vs^2$ term
with the wrong sign at the representative seed. We did not measure the
conditioning of the six-term library on either domain and assert nothing about
it. The profile interval quoted in Sec.~\ref{sec:parametric} and drawn in
Fig.~\ref{fig:ident}(b) is read at linearly interpolated $\Delta\chi^2$
crossings between the bracketing points of a $\ProfileStep$-spaced grid in
$g_{Ca}$, not at the outermost grid points of the scan; two of the five closure
estimates coincide to within a marker width, so the rug in that panel resolves
only four ticks. The parametric fit of Sec.~\ref{sec:parametric} also carries an
asymptotic relative standard error of $\valCondRelSeGcaTwoMean$ on $g_{Ca}$,
which agrees with the roughly six per cent seed spread reported there, though it
is a Gauss--Newton approximation to the same $\chi^2$ surface the profile traces
and so is not independent evidence.

\paragraph{Metric definitions.}
Two metrics carry knobs the reported numbers depend on. The rollout horizon is
the elapsed time from the first forecast sample until $|V_{\mathrm{pred}} -
V_{\mathrm{true}}|$ exceeds \Ttube\,mV and \emph{stays} above it for at least
\Rsus\,ms; at the \dtSample\,ms sampling interval that is five consecutive
samples, so a shorter excursion is discarded, a non-finite error counts as a
breach, and a trajectory that never breaches is right-censored at whichever bound
comes first: the \Rcap\,ms cap, or the time actually left in the trace after
the first forecast sample. Because the grid is \Ndata{} uniform samples over the
\Tfull\,ms window, the first forecast sample lies at \Tfcast\,ms rather than at
\Ttrain\,ms exactly, and horizons are measured from there. Spikes are local
maxima of $V$ above \Vthresh\,mV with peaks closer together than \SpikeSep\,ms
merged as refractory, matched greedily to true peaks within \SpikeTol\,ms.

\paragraph{Silent failure paths, which the tables inherit.}
A full-span solve that stops early or throws is caught and returned NaN-padded to
the full sample grid with a warning only, so a run that failed to integrate still
produces metric rows. The RMSE helper drops non-finite pairs before averaging, so
such a metric is an RMSE over whatever part of the window stayed finite and
carries no record of how much that was, which is why the diverged neural-ODE
voltage errors are reported as spans rather than as a mean and standard
deviation. During training, a step whose solve returns the wrong number of
samples is scored with a large fixed penalty instead of raising, which keeps the
optimiser moving but makes such steps indistinguishable in the loss history.
The trainer records whether the BFGS polish succeeded, but that flag reaches a
results file only for the retrain, neural-ODE parity and conductance-sweep runs;
for the headline baseline and the window and noise ablations it is discarded, so
a silent degradation to the Adam result would not be visible there. We report
this rather than repair it, because changing any of it would change the numbers.

\section{Compute, hardware and reproducibility}
\label{app:compute}

Every experiment ran on a single consumer laptop: \textbf{CPU only, no GPU,
16\,GB of RAM}, % numok: hardware, stated by the authors; nothing in the repository records it
under Julia~1.6.7 with a pinned environment, an explicit-parameter network
library \citep{pal2023lux} and a unified optimisation interface
\citep{dixit2023optimization}. The problem is small by design, a six-state ODE
closed by a $\NparamUde$-parameter multilayer perceptron fitted to $\Ntrain$
samples, so no accelerator is needed and none was used.

\textbf{Wall-clock time was never instrumented.} No timing, elapsed or duration
column exists in any results file, and no run log was kept, so we do not state a
total from memory. What the repository does record is the modification time of
each trained-parameter snapshot, which is written as its run finishes. The
conductance sweep wrote twelve such snapshots back to back, so the eleven gaps
between consecutive ones bracket eleven real trainings, and their median is
\textbf{approximately 11 minutes per UDE training run} % numok: median of the 11 consecutive-snapshot gaps in results/params/ude_abl_gca_*.jld, 6-7 Aug 2026; the 11 gaps run 10.5-16.0 min
at the Adam \AdamUde/BFGS \BfgsUde{} budget on this machine, inclusive of the
symbolic post-processing between runs. That is a measurement of elapsed time on
a shared desktop rather than of processor time, so read it as an upper bound on
the compute a reproducer needs. The aggressive-retrain runs quadruple the budget
and scale roughly in proportion. Multiplying that per-run figure by the run count
implied by the experimental design (the headline full-state and voltage-only
conditions and the neural-ODE baseline at $\valUdeForecastVRmseN$ seeds each, the
conductance sweep at $\valUdeForecastVRmseN$ seeds per point, the window and
noise ablations at a single seed per point, and the retrain control) is enough
to plan a replication. We prefer to give the ingredients than a remembered total.

\section{Remaining ablations}
\label{app:ablations}

Sec.~\ref{sec:decomp} reports the conductance sweep, which is the axis the
argument uses. Two further axes were run, each at a single seed per point with no
error bars, and neither changes any claim in the body.

% claim: window-needs-sufficient-length
\paragraph{Training window.}
Scored on the shared unseen window $t>\Tcommon$\,ms, a fifteen-millisecond
training window gives $\valWindowAblCommonEvalVRmseFifteen$\,mV, roughly seven
times the value at the twenty- and thirty-millisecond windows, which are
themselves indistinguishable ($\valWindowAblCommonEvalVRmseTwenty$ and
$\valWindowAblCommonEvalVRmseThirty$\,mV, a gap well inside the
$\pm\valCommonEvalVRmseFullStd$\,mV five-seed spread at the centre point). Too
little data fails. The forty- and fifty-millisecond windows are measurably
\emph{worse} ($\valWindowAblCommonEvalVRmseForty$ and
$\valWindowAblCommonEvalVRmseFifty$\,mV), and that non-monotonicity is
\textbf{not} evidence that longer windows are intrinsically harder: every window
trains for the same fixed iteration budget regardless of how many points it must
fit, so the longer windows are under-optimised and this axis confounds window
length with optimisation adequacy. We read it only as ``a sufficiently long
window is required, and twenty milliseconds suffices''.

% claim: noise-ablation-degrades-gracefully
\paragraph{Observation noise.}
Varying the noise level over $\{0, 1, 2, 5, 10\}$ per cent of each channel's own % numok: noise sweep axis, experiments_runner.jl:44; the 2 per cent baseline point is B_NOISE, experiments_runner.jl:49
standard deviation, with every other axis at baseline, the normalised calcium
error rises monotonically through $\valNoiseAblICaNormZero$,
$\valNoiseAblICaNormOnePct$, $\valNoiseAblICaNormTwoPct$,
$\valNoiseAblICaNormFivePct$ and $\valNoiseAblICaNormTenPct$, and forecast
voltage error from $\valNoiseAblVRmseZero$\,mV to
$\valNoiseAblVRmseTenPct$\,mV. The zero-noise point is a control the paper
otherwise lacks: with no observation noise at all the closure's calcium error
does not vanish but falls only to about a tenth of its baseline value, so part of
what the closure gets wrong is its own approximation error and not the data's
noise. The degradation is gentle across the lower four levels and steep only at
the top, which puts the \NoisePct\,\% setting used everywhere else well inside
the flat part of the range. This is a single-seed axis, so we read the
end-to-end trend and not the individual steps. Unlike the window sweep, it
cannot borrow the five-seed spread at its centre point as a yardstick, because
the noise level is the very quantity that sets seed-to-seed variation.
