\begin{abstract}
Universal differential equations (UDEs) embed a neural network inside a
mechanistic model to learn a term the modeller does not know. In
conductance-based neuron models the natural application is to learn an unknown
ionic current from an observed voltage trace and then read the recovered
current's coefficients as biophysical estimates. We test whether that reading is
earned.
% claim: single-trajectory-is-the-binding-limitation
We replace the calcium current in a six-state Hodgkin--Huxley model with a
neural closure $U(V,s)$, train on a single \Tfull\,ms noisy trajectory, and
distil the closure back into a conductance form $s^2(aV+b)$.
% claim: ude-forecasts-accurately
% claim: node-diverges
% claim: voltage-only-equivalent
The UDE forecasts held-out voltage to $\valUdeForecastVRmseMeanStd$\,mV
(mean\,$\pm$\,SD over $\valUdeForecastVRmseN$ seeds) where a black-box neural ODE
leaves a \Ttube\,mV error tube after $\valNodeForecastRolloutMeanStd$\,ms;
restricting the loss to voltage alone matches full-state observation to within
seed noise.
% claim: symbolic-form-recovered
% claim: symbolic-coefficients-not-identifiable
The closure recovers the current's functional form
($R^2 = \valSymbolicRTwoCondTrueMeanStd$ against the true current) but not its
scale: $\hat{a} = \valSymbolicAHatMeanStd$ against a true $\gcaMain$, with a
per-seed span of \valSymbolicAHatRange{} that brackets the truth on both sides.
% claim: retrain-does-not-close-gap
This is not under-training. Quadrupling the optimisation budget leaves every run
converged yet makes the estimate worse and noisier: different closures fit the
same voltage equally well. From one trajectory, the calcium current's shape is
identifiable and its conductance is not. Code, trained parameters, and all
results tables are released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Motivation: partially-known dynamics in conductance-based models}

Conductance-based neuron models are the workhorse of computational
neurophysiology, and they are almost never fully known. A modeller typically
has confident descriptions of the well-characterised currents (the fast sodium
and delayed-rectifier potassium currents of \citet{hodgkin1952}, plus a leak), and a
much weaker grip on the slower, modulatory currents that shape bursting and
adaptation. Universal differential equations
\citep{rackauckas2020universal}, mechanistic differential equations with a
neural network standing in for an unknown term, are an attractive way to close
that gap: keep the physics you trust, and learn the physics you do not from data.

The appeal is not only predictive. Because a UDE isolates the unknown term as a
single learned function, it invites a second step: distil that function back
into a closed form and read its coefficients as biophysical quantities. In a
conductance-based model those coefficients are a conductance and a reversal
potential. This two-stage recipe, fitting a network inside the ODE and then
running symbolic regression on it \citep{brunton2016sindy}, is exactly what a
practitioner reaching for interpretable mechanism would do, and it is what the
foundational UDE work proposes.

\subsection{The gap: from prediction to mechanism, and when it fails}

Reading a coefficient off a learned closure presumes that the data determine it.
That presumption is rarely tested. A UDE is usually judged by the diagnostics of
a predictive model: rollout error, a parity plot of the learned term against
ground truth. Both can look excellent while the coefficient the modeller
actually wants is not pinned down at all. Neuroscience has known for two decades
that conductance-based models are degenerate: very different parameter sets
produce indistinguishable activity \citep{prinz2004,marder2011}, and the gating
variables and conductances of a generalised Hodgkin--Huxley model are not
separately identifiable from voltage \citep{walch2016}. That knowledge has not
been carried into the equation-discovery literature, which reports recovery
successes without asking whether recovery was possible in principle.

We study one instance of this gap in full. We take an advanced Hodgkin--Huxley
neuron (the classical model augmented with a persistent sodium current and a
high-threshold calcium current), hide the calcium current behind a neural
closure, and run the whole predict-then-interpret pipeline against ground truth
we control. The failure mode we find is the \emph{default practice}: fitting a
UDE to a single trajectory and reading off the learned current's coefficients is
exactly what a practitioner would reasonably do, and on this system it recovers
the current's shape while getting its scale wrong by a factor that varies across
random seeds. The contribution is not that this can happen; it is a
characterisation of \emph{when} it happens, and a demonstration, with two
controls, that the failure is a property of the data rather than of the fit or
the optimiser.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item % claim: ude-forecasts-accurately
  \textbf{A reproducible advanced-HH SciML benchmark.} Embedding the known
  physics and learning only the calcium current turns a \Rcap\,ms voltage
  extrapolation from impossible into routine: a black-box neural ODE leaves a
  \Ttube\,mV error tube $\valNodeForecastRolloutMeanStd$\,ms past the training
  boundary, while the UDE never breaches it
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-forecasting}, Fig.~\ref{fig:node-vs-ude}).

  \item % claim: voltage-only-equivalent
  % claim: physics-prior-reconstructs-unobserved-gates
  \textbf{A physics prior that reconstructs the unobserved state.} Observing
  \emph{only} voltage in the loss matches full-state observation to within seed
  noise, and the five gate trajectories, which never enter the objective, are
  reconstructed anyway by the retained kinetics
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-partial}, Fig.~\ref{fig:voltage-only}).

  \item % claim: symbolic-form-recovered
  % claim: symbolic-coefficients-not-identifiable
  \textbf{Shape recovered, scale not.} The learned closure recovers the
  \emph{functional form} of the calcium current
  ($R^2 = \valSymbolicRTwoCondTrueMeanStd$ against the true current) but not its
  \emph{scale}: $\hat{a} = \valSymbolicAHatMeanStd$ against a true value of
  $\gcaMain$, with a per-seed span of \valSymbolicAHatRange{} that brackets the
  truth on both sides (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-symbolic}, Fig.~\ref{fig:symbolic}).

  \item % claim: retrain-does-not-close-gap
  \textbf{The gap is structural, not optimisational.} An aggressive-retrain
  control establishes that the coefficient gap is a property of the problem and
  not of our training: quadrupling the budget leaves every run converged and
  makes the estimate \emph{worse and noisier}
  ($\hat{a} = \valRetrainAHatMeanStd$), while the identical pipeline recovers the
  true coefficients when fit against ground truth on the same domain
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-retrain}, Table~\ref{tab:retrain}).

  \item % claim: gca-sweep-shows-nonidentifiability
  \textbf{Locating the boundary.} A conductance sweep locates where recovery
  becomes possible: normalised calcium error falls from
  $\valGcaAblICaNormPointFour$ at the physiological value $g_{Ca}=\gcaPhys$ (where
  the closure's error exceeds the calcium signal's own variation) to
  $\valGcaAblICaNormFour$ at $g_{Ca}=\gcaHi$
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-ablations}, Fig.~\ref{fig:gca}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Hybrid modelling: UDEs and neural ODEs.}
Neural ordinary differential equations parameterise a vector field with a network
\citep{chen2018neural}; universal differential equations generalise this by
placing a network inside an otherwise mechanistic model, keeping known structure
and learning only the unknown terms \citep{rackauckas2020universal}, an approach
adjacent to physics-informed neural networks \citep{raissi2019pinn}. The
technique is powerful, but its recovery guarantees are empirical rather than
theoretical, and the field is only now mapping where it fails.
\citet{silvestri2023analysis} analyse when UDEs recover the right dynamics, and
\citet{philipps2025ude} catalogue open problems: noise and limited data degrade
UDE performance, and interpretability requires explicit regularisation. This is
precisely the regime we probe.

\paragraph{From a learned function to a closed form.}
The second stage of the pipeline, distilling a learned function into an equation,
is standard: sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics
\citep{brunton2016sindy} and its variants \citep{rudy2017pdefind, zheng2019sr3,
fasel2022ensemblesindy, desilva2020pysindy}, and genetic or physics-inspired
symbolic regression \citep{cranmer2023pysr, udrescu2020aifeynman}. Every one of
these methods assumes the learned function is determined by the data. That
assumption is exactly what we test: we run sparse regression on a closure whose
predictions match ground truth well, and report what happens when the assumption
does not hold.

\paragraph{Conductance-based models and their currents.}
The advanced model we use is the squid-axon Hodgkin--Huxley set
\citep{hodgkin1952} in the modern minimal-model tradition \citep{pospischil2008},
augmented with a persistent sodium current (activation from
\citet{magistretti1999}, conductance range from \citet{golomb1997}) and a
high-threshold calcium current (kinetics and reversal potential from
\citet{reuveni1993}). Every parameter we vary or hold fixed is grounded in this
literature or declared as a modelling choice; the parameter table
(Sec.~\ref{sec:setup-model}) makes the provenance explicit, because the first
objection to any non-identifiability result is that the parameters were tuned to
produce it.

\paragraph{Degeneracy and identifiability.}
Two threads converge on our result. In neuroscience, disparate parameter sets
are long known to produce near-identical activity \citep{prinz2004},
model-averaging can fail outright \citep{golowasch2002}, families of
indistinguishable models are the rule rather than the exception
\citep{marder2011}, and the same holds under parametric variation of the
Hodgkin--Huxley model itself \citep{ori2018}. In systems identification,
structural and practical identifiability have precise definitions and diagnostics
\citep{raue2009, wieland2021}; \citet{walch2016} apply them to generalised
Hodgkin--Huxley models and prove that gating variables and conductances form
identifiable \emph{combinations} rather than being separately identifiable, and
\citet{daly2015} reach a compatible conclusion by approximate Bayesian analysis.
Our result is continuous with this body of work and imports it into the SciML
setting.

\paragraph{SciML applied to neurons, and the gap.}
Neural and universal differential equations have been applied to
conductance-based neurons for parameter and state estimation
\citep{meliza2014, burghi2021feedback, iravanian2020}, and UDEs have been
proposed as a unifying modelling language for the field \citep{elgazzar2025ude}.
Closest to our setting, \citet{beck2026hybrid} embed neural ODEs in
conductance-based models to learn unknown currents and misspecified kinetics,
validating across a large channel database. They own the claim that one can put a
network inside a Hodgkin--Huxley model and learn a hidden current, and we do not
contest it. What remains open is what they do not do: they do not test calcium,
do not run symbolic regression (they name it as future work), and treat
underdetermination as a one-sentence aside. \citet{loman2025ude} supply the
vocabulary we use, parametric versus functional identifiability, and a tension
we address directly: their headline is that inserting a network into a
mechanistic ODE has minimal impact on the identifiability of the traditional
parameters, whereas here it is the crux. Prior work establishes that neural
components can be embedded in conductance-based models and that such models are
degenerate; no one has asked \emph{which} hidden current is recoverable, from
\emph{how much} of a single trajectory, and whether failures are optimisational
or structural.

\section{Background and Problem Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{The advanced Hodgkin--Huxley model}
\label{sec:setup-model}

The system is a single-compartment neuron with state
$u = (V, m, h, n, p, s)$: membrane potential $V$, the classical sodium
activation/inactivation gates $m, h$, the potassium activation gate $n$, a
persistent-sodium gate $p$, and a calcium gate $s$. The membrane equation sums
five ionic currents against an applied drive,
\begin{equation}
C_m \frac{dV}{dt} = I_{\mathrm{app}} - I_K - I_{Na} - I_L - I_{NaP} - I_{Ca},
\qquad
I_{Ca} = g_{Ca}\, s^2\, (V - E_{Ca}),
\label{eq:membrane}
\end{equation}
with $I_K = g_K n^4 (V-E_K)$, $I_{Na} = g_{Na} m^3 h (V-E_{Na})$,
$I_L = g_L(V-E_L)$, and $I_{NaP} = g_{NaP}\, p\, (V-E_{Na})$. Each gate $x$ obeys
first-order kinetics $\dot{x} = \alpha_x(V)(1-x) - \beta_x(V)x$; the classical
$\alpha,\beta$ functions and the persistent-sodium and calcium steady-state
activations are given in Appendix~\ref{app:eqns}. The trajectory is a repetitive
spiking limit cycle driven by a constant $I_{\mathrm{app}}$.

The calcium current is the target of the study. It is a squared-gate current with
a linear driving force, so a conductance and a reversal potential are the two
biophysical quantities a successful recovery must return: in \eqref{eq:membrane},
$g_{Ca}$ is the conductance and $E_{Ca}$ the reversal potential, and the two
appear in the equivalent form $I_{Ca} = s^2(aV+b)$ with $a=g_{Ca}$ and
$b=-g_{Ca}E_{Ca}$.

Table~\ref{tab:params} lists every constant with its provenance. Sodium and
potassium kinetics and the passive parameters use the standard squid-axon values
\citep{hodgkin1952} in the modern resting-potential-referenced convention; the
persistent-sodium conductance and half-activation, and the calcium half-activation
and reversal potential, follow \citet{golomb1997}, \citet{magistretti1999}, and
\citet{reuveni1993}. The remaining values are declared modelling choices, not
measurements. The calcium conductance $g_{Ca}$ is treated separately: it is not a
model constant but a builder argument and the axis of the identifiability
experiment of Sec.~\ref{sec:results-ablations}, set to $\gcaMain$ for the
headline runs and swept down to the physiological $\gcaPhys$; we return to that
choice below and in Sec.~\ref{sec:setup-hidden}.

\subsection{Known physics versus the hidden current: the recovery task}
\label{sec:setup-hidden}

The recovery task is deliberately favourable to the method. All four other
currents and all six gate kinetics, including the calcium gate's own dynamics
$\dot s$, are retained exactly; only the algebraic term $I_{Ca}$ in
\eqref{eq:membrane} is unknown and must be learned as a function of $(V,s)$. The
gate $s$ is therefore available to the closure as an input because \emph{its
kinetics are known physics}, not because calcium is observed. The two unknowns
are the conductance $g_{Ca}$ and the reversal potential $E_{Ca}$; the functional
form $s^2(V-E_{Ca})$ is what the symbolic stage is asked to rediscover.

% claim: gca-two-is-a-controlled-choice
The main experiments use $g_{Ca}=\gcaMain$, above the physiological value of
about $\gcaPhys$. This is a deliberate choice, not a convenience, and we defend
it as an experiment rather than a setting: $g_{Ca}$ controls how much the calcium
current contributes to the voltage the loss can see, so it \emph{is} an
identifiability axis. We sweep it in full (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-ablations}),
report the value where the method fails hardest, and make that failure part of
the result. At the physiological value the calcium current is too small a
contribution to the voltage to be recoverable at all; $\gcaMain$ is the regime in
which recovery is possible enough to be interesting, which is exactly why the
residual coefficient gap there is a finding rather than a shrug.

\subsection{Data generation and evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:setup-data}

For each run we solve the true model over $[0,\Tfull]$\,ms, sample $\Ndata$
points, and add temporally-correlated Ornstein--Uhlenbeck noise to every channel,
each channel's noise renormalised to unit variance and then scaled to a fixed
fraction of that channel's standard deviation. Models are trained on the noisy
data over $t\le\Ttrain$\,ms and evaluated against the \emph{clean} trajectory, so
error measures recovery of the underlying dynamics rather than memorisation of
noise; a secondary error against the noisy target records the noise floor. The
forecast window is $t>\Ttrain$\,ms of a single uninterrupted integration from the
true initial condition; the model is never re-initialised at the train/forecast
boundary. For the training-window ablation, where the forecast window would
otherwise move with the training length, every length is additionally scored on a
fixed \emph{common} window $t>\Tcommon$\,ms that is unseen for all of them.

\subsection{Black-box Neural ODE baseline}

The baseline is a black-box neural ODE \citep{chen2018neural} that replaces the
entire right-hand side with a network of the six normalised states and time,
trained on the same data. It carries no physics and serves only to establish the
scale of the forecasting problem: what ``the UDE extrapolates'' means depends on
what a model without the physics prior does on the same task. Architecture and
budgets are in Appendix~\ref{app:hyper}.

\subsection{Universal differential equation with embedded physics}

The UDE is byte-identical to the true right-hand side except for one line: the
algebraic term $I_{Ca}=g_{Ca}s^2(V-E_{Ca})$ is replaced by
$U(V,s)=\mathrm{net}(\NNinputs;\theta)$, a small multilayer perceptron
(two hidden layers of width sixteen, $\NparamUde$ parameters). Everything
else (the four known currents, all six gate ODEs, $I_{\mathrm{app}}$, $C_m$, the
reversal potentials) is the exact physics. The network never sees $g_{Ca}$; that
constant shapes only the true data being fitted.

The observation condition is a one-line change to the loss. In the full-state
condition the squared error is summed over all six states; in the
\emph{voltage-only} condition it is summed over the voltage row alone, so
$m,h,n,p,s$ never enter the objective. In both conditions the solver still
integrates all six states from the full true initial condition, so ``voltage
only'' refers to observation in the loss, not to what is known about the initial
state or evolved by the physics.

\subsection{Symbolic recovery of the learned current}
\label{sec:methods-symbolic}

Given a trained closure $U$, we recover an equation for it in two complementary
ways, both fit on a grid of $(V,s)$ points restricted to the convex hull of the
training trajectory's $(V,s)$ cloud. The restriction matters: the spiking limit
cycle is a near-one-dimensional loop in $(V,s)$, so fitting on the trajectory
alone is rank-deficient and the terms are not separable; the hull-masked grid is
the network's genuine two-dimensional interpolation region.

The first recovery is unconstrained sparse regression over the low-order library
$\{1, V, s, Vs, s^2, Vs^2\}$, which contains the truth exactly, by standardised
sequentially-thresholded least squares followed by ordinary-least-squares
debiasing, in the manner of SINDy \citep{brunton2016sindy}. The second is a
structure-informed fit to the biophysical conductance form $s^2(aV+b)$, whose
coefficients map directly to the physics: $a=g_{Ca}$, $b=-g_{Ca}E_{Ca}$, and the
reversal potential is $-b/a$. As an exoneration control we apply the identical
estimator, on the identical hull-restricted domain, to the \emph{analytic} true
current; this checks the regression rather than the network. Recovered forms are
scored against the true current on the trajectory.

\subsection{Training, metrics, and reproducibility}
\label{sec:methods-train}

% claim: forecast-is-one-uninterrupted-rollout
All models are integrated with an explicit Runge--Kutta method
\citep{rackauckas2017diffeq} at tight tolerances, trained by Adam followed by an
L-BFGS polish under a continuous adjoint \citep{ma2021comparison,
rackauckas2020universal}, with the network parameterised explicitly
\citep{pal2023lux} and the optimisation driven through a unified interface
\citep{dixit2023optimization}; exact budgets are in Appendix~\ref{app:hyper}.
Forecast error is the tail of a single continuous integration from the true
initial condition (extrapolation error, not one-step-ahead error), because the
model is never re-initialised at the boundary.

Every headline metric is a mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation over
$\valUdeForecastVRmseN$ seeds; ablations are single runs at the pre-registered
baseline seed. A seed jointly redraws \emph{both} the network initialisation and
the Ornstein--Uhlenbeck noise realisation, so the reported spread is a joint
init-and-data spread and the two sources cannot be separated from these runs; we
report it as such and run no statistical test.
% claim: rep-seed-is-guarded-not-cherry-picked
The single seed used for the trajectory and parity panels is the pre-registered
baseline, fixed before the seed spread was known, and a guardrail confirms it is
median-typical (within the inter-quartile range of the five-seed
distribution) rather than selected from it.

% claim: calcium-error-is-decoupled-from-trajectory-error
Two error families are reported and must not be conflated. Voltage error
accumulates over a free rollout. The calcium-current error probes the learned
closure at the true clean $(V,s)$ and is therefore a function-approximation
error, decoupled from trajectory error; a normalised version divides it by the
true current's standard deviation within the window, so a value above one means
the closure's error exceeds the signal it is meant to reproduce. It is precisely
because these two are separable that ``accurate voltage, wrong coefficients'' is
coherent rather than contradictory.

\subsection{Forecasting: the neural ODE diverges, the UDE extrapolates}
\label{sec:results-forecasting}

Both models are trained on $t\le\Ttrain$\,ms of one noisy trajectory and then
integrated freely to \Tfull\,ms from the true initial condition; the forecast
window is the tail of one uninterrupted rollout.
% claim: node-diverges
The black-box neural ODE leaves the \Ttube\,mV error tube
$\valNodeForecastRolloutMeanStd$\,ms after the training boundary (mean $\pm$ SD,
$n=\valNodeForecastRolloutN$ seeds), with two of the five seeds breaching at the
first forecast sample. Its forecast voltage error is not a quantity we can
summarise: the integration diverges rather than degrades, spanning
$\NodeVspan$\,mV across seeds on a variable whose physiological range is roughly
one hundred millivolts, and even those values are computed over the finite part of
a blown-up solve.
We therefore report the divergence categorically and quote the rollout horizon,
which stays well defined in the failure regime.

% claim: ude-forecasts-accurately
The UDE, which differs from the neural ODE only in what it is asked to
learn, one scalar current rather than six derivatives, never breaches the tube.
Its rollout horizon saturates the metric's cap for every seed
($\valUdeForecastRollout$\,ms; the cap is \Rcap\,ms, the entire remaining
trajectory, so the horizon is right-censored and its true value is unknown), and
its forecast voltage RMSE is $\valUdeForecastVRmseMeanStd$\,mV.
% claim: ude-learns-true-dynamics-not-noise
The fit is not memorisation of the training window: the UDE's training-window
error against the clean trajectory, $\valUdeTrainVRmseMeanStd$\,mV, is well below
its error against the noisy signal it was actually fit to,
$\valUdeTrainVRmseVsNoisyMeanStd$\,mV. It therefore recovered the underlying dynamics
rather than the noise. The two models' budgets are not equal (the neural ODE
trains for fewer Adam iterations, Table~\ref{tab:forecast}), and we do not think
the asymmetry is load-bearing: a modest increase in iterations does not plausibly
bridge the gap between breaching at $\valNodeForecastRolloutMean$\,ms and not
breaching in \Rcap\,ms. What this establishes is narrow and worth stating
narrowly. Retaining the known currents and learning only the calcium term
converts a \Rcap\,ms extrapolation from impossible to routine. It does not
establish that the learned term \emph{is} the calcium current, which is the
subject of Sec.~\ref{sec:results-symbolic}.

\subsection{Partial observation: the voltage-only UDE}
\label{sec:results-partial}

% claim: physics-prior-reconstructs-unobserved-gates
Restricting the loss to the voltage trace alone removes the five gate variables
from the objective entirely; they are never observed, never scored, and never
supervised. They are nonetheless reconstructed to a mean gate RMSE of
$\valUdeVoltageForecastGateRmseMeanStd$ over the forecast window. Nothing in the
network learned them; the retained gate kinetics did, once the learned
$I_{Ca}(V,s)$ was constrained enough by the voltage fit to keep the vector field
on the manifold. This is the mechanistic prior's contribution made visible
(Fig.~\ref{fig:voltage-only}), and it is a positive result inside a paper whose
headline is a limit.

% claim: voltage-only-equivalent
Restricting the loss to voltage also matches full-state observation to within
seed noise on the quantities that can be compared: forecast $V$ RMSE
$\valUdeVoltageForecastVRmseMeanStd$ versus $\valUdeForecastVRmseMeanStd$\,mV, and
forecast $I_{Ca}$ RMSE $\valUdeVoltageForecastICaRmseMeanStd$ versus
$\valUdeForecastICaRmseMeanStd$\,\nicefrac{$\mu$A}{cm$^2$}
($n=\valUdeForecastVRmseN$; no significance test is possible at this sample size
and none is claimed). We do not rank the two conditions: the differences are well
inside the seed spread, and the finding of interest is the equivalence
itself: one can drop five of six observed channels from the loss for free,
because the physics prior, not the additional supervision, supplies the hidden
states.

\subsection{Ablations: training window and calcium identifiability}
\label{sec:results-ablations}

% claim: gca-sweep-shows-nonidentifiability
The conductance sweep is the quantitative backbone of the identifiability claim
(Fig.~\ref{fig:gca}). Sweeping $g_{Ca}\in\{\gcaPhys,\gcaMid,\gcaMain,\gcaHi\}$ with every
other axis pinned, the normalised calcium error falls monotonically:
$\valGcaAblICaNormPointFour$, $\valGcaAblICaNormOne$, $\valGcaAblICaNormTwo$,
$\valGcaAblICaNormFour$. % numok: gCa sweep axis values, experiments_runner.jl:34
Signal amplitude is the only thing that changes across this axis (same
architecture, same data volume, same optimiser, same iterations), so it is an
identifiability axis by construction. At the physiological $g_{Ca}=\gcaPhys$ the
normalised error exceeds one: the closure's error is larger than the calcium
current's own variation, i.e.\ the network has learned essentially nothing about
a current that small, while still forecasting voltage well. Judged against the
five-seed spread at the shared centre point ($\pm\valUdeForecastICaRmseNormStd$),
the collapse from $\gcaPhys$ to $\gcaMid$ is far outside seed noise; the difference
between $\gcaMain$ and $\gcaHi$ is comparable to it, and we draw no conclusion from
that step. At $g_{Ca}=\gcaPhys$ the symbolic fit additionally returns the
\emph{wrong sign} for the conductance ($\hat a = \valSymbolicAHatNegControl$
against a true $+\gcaPhys$), a harder failure than its $R^2$ conveys.

% claim: window-needs-sufficient-length
The training-window sweep, scored on the shared unseen window
$t>\Tcommon$\,ms, shows that too little data fails: a fifteen-millisecond window
gives $\valWindowAblCommonEvalVRmseFifteen$\,mV, roughly seven times the value at
any longer window, because that window ends inside the initial transient and never
observes a full limit cycle. Beyond twenty milliseconds the axis is flat to within
seed noise ($\valWindowAblCommonEvalVRmseTwenty$,
$\valWindowAblCommonEvalVRmseThirty$, $\valWindowAblCommonEvalVRmseForty$,
$\valWindowAblCommonEvalVRmseFifty$\,mV against a seed spread of
$\pm\valUdeForecastVRmseStd$ at the centre point, single seed per point). The mild
upward drift at the two longest windows is \emph{not} evidence that longer windows
are harder: every window trains for the same fixed budget regardless of how many
points it must fit, so the longer windows are under-optimised. We read this axis
as ``a sufficiently long window is required, and twenty milliseconds suffices,''
and draw no conclusion from its non-monotonicity.

\subsection{Symbolic recovery of the calcium current}
\label{sec:results-symbolic}

% claim: symbolic-form-recovered
Constrained to the biophysical conductance form $s^2(aV+b)$, the distilled
closure reproduces the true calcium current with
$R^2 = \valSymbolicRTwoCondTrueMeanStd$ against the analytic ground truth
($n=\valUdeForecastVRmseN$, per-seed mean $\pm$ SD). The \emph{functional form} is
recovered: the current is a squared-gate term with a linear driving force, and
the fit says so (Fig.~\ref{fig:symbolic}a).

% claim: symbolic-coefficients-not-identifiable
The \emph{coefficients} are not. The fitted conductance is
$\hat a = \valSymbolicAHatMeanStd$ against a true value of $\gcaMain$, with a standard
deviation more than half the mean, and the per-seed estimates span
\valSymbolicAHatRange, a factor of more than four, bracketing the truth on both
sides (Fig.~\ref{fig:symbolic}b). The offset $\hat b = \valSymbolicBHatMeanStd$
is closer in relative terms but carries a comparable spread. These are five
different closures that happen to fit the same voltage, not five measurements of
one conductance; the interval that contains the truth is wide enough to contain
almost anything, and we call the conductance order-correct, not recovered.

% claim: regression-machinery-is-sound
The gap is the network's, not the regression's. Applying the identical estimator,
on the identical hull-restricted domain, to the \emph{analytic} calcium current
returns $a\approx\gcaMain$ and $b\approx\bTrue$ exactly, the true values. % numok: sanity fit console output, objective3_symbolic.jl:208-209
The estimator, the fitting domain, and the convex-hull mask all recover the truth
when the truth is what they are shown, so the spread in $\hat a$ is a property of
the trained closure, not of the fit.

% claim: sindy-does-not-sparsify
The unconstrained discovery attempt tells the same story from the opposite
direction. Sparse regression over the six-term library retains every term at
every seed ($\valSindyNTermsRetained$), never producing a sparse equation, yet
the resulting fit still predicts the true current well
($R^2 = \valSymbolicRTwoSindyTrueMeanStd$). At the representative seed the
physical $Vs^2$ term is even fitted with the \emph{wrong sign} while that same fit
scores a high $R^2$ against the true current. This is the cleanest single
illustration that predictive accuracy and identifiability are different properties. The
network's small departures from the exact form leak into the correlated low-order
terms; this is an identifiability limit, not a defect of the sparse solver.

\subsection{Is the residual gap trainable? An aggressive-retrain control}
\label{sec:results-retrain}

% claim: retrain-does-not-close-gap
The obvious objection is that the coefficient gap is under-training. It is not,
and the control that shows so is the strongest single result in the study
(Table~\ref{tab:retrain}). We quadrupled the optimisation budget, to \AdamRe{}
Adam and \BfgsRe{} L-BFGS iterations with everything else identical, and re-ran the
same symbolic pipeline. The optimiser converged: L-BFGS succeeded on all five
seeds and the final losses are tight ($\valRetrainFinalLossMeanStd$). And yet
every recovery quantity got \emph{worse and noisier}: the conductance moved
farther from the truth and its standard deviation inflated
($\hat a$: $\valSymbolicAHatMeanStd \to \valRetrainAHatMeanStd$), agreement with
the true current fell
($R^2$: $\valSymbolicRTwoCondTrueMeanStd \to \valRetrainRTwoCondTrueMeanStd$), and
even the trajectory forecast the loss actually optimises degraded
($\valUdeForecastVRmseMeanStd \to \valRetrainForecastVRmseMeanStd$\,mV). More
optimisation did not find a better closure; it found more different closures that
fit the same voltage equally well, which is what non-identifiability looks like
from inside an optimiser: the objective is flat in the direction we are trying to
read.

% claim: retrain-seed-decoupling
The representative seed makes the mechanism concrete. Under the aggressive
retrain its forecast voltage accuracy is essentially unchanged
($\valBaselineRepSeedForecastVRmse \to \valRetrainRepSeedForecastVRmse$\,mV, well
within the five-seed baseline spread), while its agreement with the true calcium
current collapses
($R^2$: $\valBaselineRepSeedRTwoCondTrue \to \valRetrainRepSeedRTwoCondTrue$). The
same trajectory quality is achieved by a different, and no better, closure:
trajectory quality and closure identity moved independently, which is exactly
what a flat direction in the loss means, observed directly rather than inferred.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

% claim: ude-recovers-trajectory-not-coefficients
The picture assembled across these experiments is a single, scoped finding: the
UDE recovers the trajectory and the functional form of the hidden calcium current
but not its coefficients, and the residual gap is a property of the data. Voltage
is forecast to sub-millivolt accuracy over the whole un-trained remainder; the
closure distils to the correct conductance form; and the conductance itself is
only order-correct, with neither a signal-amplitude sweep nor a fourfold
optimisation budget closing the gap. What turns this from a failure report into a
characterisation is the set of controls: the estimator recovers the truth from
the analytic current, so the fault is not the regression; the gap widens toward
low conductance in a controlled sweep, so it is not an accident of the headline
setting; and it survives, indeed grows, under quadrupled optimisation with a
converged optimiser, so it is not under-training.

% claim: predictive-accuracy-is-not-identifiability
The transferable lesson is that predictive accuracy on the observed channel is
not evidence that a learned physical term has been identified. Three independent
instances make the point here: sparse regression attains a high $R^2$ against the
true current while fitting its dominant term with the wrong sign; five seeds reach
indistinguishable forecast accuracy with conductances spanning more than a factor
of four; and the retrain holds forecast accuracy on the representative seed while
its agreement with the true current collapses. Parity plots and rollout error,
the two diagnostics UDE studies most often report, are exactly the diagnostics
that cannot see this failure. Our result is continuous with the
identifiable-combinations theory of \citet{walch2016}: the network pins the
product that determines the current's contribution to $dV/dt$, not the
factorisation into a conductance and a gating shape, and a flexible closure
absorbs that ambiguity rather than resolving it.

% claim: identifiability-is-the-general-lesson
We state the general form of this as a recommendation rather than a theorem,
because our evidence is one system. Where a UDE's learned term is meant to be
interpreted rather than merely simulated, its identifiability from the available
data should be established before its coefficients are read as physical
quantities; a single trajectory can be sufficient to fit and insufficient to
identify. The cheapest diagnostic is the one that would have caught the failure
here: a seed-spread check on the recovered coefficients. The mechanism we
observe, a flat direction in the loss along which the closure varies while the
observable does not, is general, but its presence in any particular system is a
hypothesis to be tested, not assumed. The choice of $g_{Ca}=\gcaMain$ was made in
that spirit: not to make the method succeed, but to place the system in the regime
where recovery is possible enough that its residual failure is informative.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

% claim: single-trajectory-is-the-binding-limitation
The binding limitation is that every conclusion rests on one trajectory: one
applied drive, one initial condition, one \Tfull\,ms window. Every
identifiability statement is therefore ``not identifiable \emph{from this
trajectory}.'' The natural next experiment is whether multiple trajectories
spanning different initial conditions and stimulus amplitudes restore
identifiability of the calcium conductance; the identifiable-combinations analysis
of \citet{walch2016} predicts which additional measurement would break the
degeneracy, which turns this negative result into an experimental-design
recommendation. We do not run that experiment and make no prediction about its
outcome. Three further limitations bound the claims. All ablations are single-seed
and carry no error bars; we calibrate them against the five-seed spread at the
shared centre point and claim only the gaps that clear it. No statistical test is
run anywhere, and $n=\valUdeForecastVRmseN$ would support almost none; every
comparative statement is an equivalence-within-noise statement, not an ordering.
And we provide no positive control showing that \emph{adding} information closes
the gap (the experiment a reader would most want) because the evidence base for
this study is closed; it is named here rather than wished away. Separately, the
seeds redraw the network initialisation and the noise realisation jointly, so the
reported spread cannot be decomposed into the two.

% =========================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Embedding known physics and learning one hidden ionic current turns an impossible
extrapolation into a routine one and recovers the functional form of the calcium
current, even when only voltage is observed. It does not recover the current's
conductance from a single trajectory, and the failure is structural: the
regression is exonerated by a control, the signal-amplitude sweep locates the
boundary, and quadrupled optimisation makes the estimate worse rather than better.
The practical consequence for scientific machine learning is a caution with a
concrete remedy: a UDE coefficient is a physical estimate only when the data
identify it, an inexpensive seed-spread check will flag when they do not, and the
identifiable-combinations literature says which additional measurement to collect
when they do.

\section{Model equations and gating kinetics}

The classical gating kinetics are (constants verbatim from the cited source; each
line names its provenance):
\begin{align*}
  \alpha_m(V) &= \frac{0.1\,(V+40)}{1-e^{-(V+40)/10}}, &  % numok: HH kinetics, src/hh_core.jl:46-59
  \beta_m(V)  &= 4\,e^{-(V+65)/18}, \\                    % numok: HH kinetics, src/hh_core.jl:46-59
  \alpha_h(V) &= 0.07\,e^{-(V+65)/20}, &                  % numok: HH kinetics, src/hh_core.jl:46-59
  \beta_h(V)  &= \frac{1}{1+e^{-(V+35)/10}}, \\           % numok: HH kinetics, src/hh_core.jl:46-59
  \alpha_n(V) &= \frac{0.01\,(V+55)}{1-e^{-(V+55)/10}}, & % numok: HH kinetics, src/hh_core.jl:46-59
  \beta_n(V)  &= 0.125\,e^{-(V+65)/80},                   % numok: HH kinetics, src/hh_core.jl:46-59
\end{align*}
with the removable singularities of $\alpha_m,\alpha_n$ evaluated at their
analytic limits. The two added gates use steady-state activations $p_\infty(V) = [1+e^{-(V+50)/6}]^{-1}$ and $s_\infty(V) = [1+e^{-(V+25)/9}]^{-1}$ % numok: p_inf/s_inf, src/hh_core.jl:61,66
with first-order relaxation at fixed time constants $\tau_p,\tau_s$
(Table~\ref{tab:params}); the persistent-sodium activation half-point follows
\citet{magistretti1999} and the calcium activation half-point and slope follow
\citet{reuveni1993}.

\section{Full hyperparameters and training details}
\label{app:hyper}

The UDE calcium closure is a multilayer perceptron
$\ArchUde$ with $\tanh$ activations and inputs $\NNinputs$
($\NparamUde$ parameters); the black-box neural ODE is $\ArchNode$ with layer
normalisation and inputs of the six normalised states and raw time
($\approx\NparamNode$ parameters).
Both are integrated with an explicit fifth-order Runge--Kutta method at absolute
and relative tolerances of $\Tol$, under an interpolating continuous adjoint with
reverse-mode vector--Jacobian products. Training is two-stage: Adam at learning
rate $\Lr$ followed by an L-BFGS polish (initial step-norm $\Lr$) wrapped so that
a failed polish degrades to the Adam result. Budgets are Adam \AdamUde/L-BFGS
\BfgsUde{} for the UDE and Adam \AdamNode/L-BFGS \BfgsNode{} for the neural ODE;
the aggressive-retrain control of Sec.~\ref{sec:results-retrain} uses Adam
\AdamRe/L-BFGS \BfgsRe. The five seeds are $\{1111,2222,3333,4444,5555\}$; % numok: seed set, experiments_runner.jl:45
the representative seed for single-run panels is the first of these, verified
median-typical by the guardrail of Sec.~\ref{sec:methods-train}.
